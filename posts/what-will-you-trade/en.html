Every song on Through Their Eyes, Vol. 1 tells a hero's story through the eyes of someone who watched them up close. For the Elric brothers of Fullmetal Alchemist, there was only one possible witness: the one who took their bodies. Truth, the being behind the Gate.

The idea: start where it hurts

Most songs on the album open with energy. This one had to open with a loss: two boys, a chalk circle on a cellar floor, and a transmutation that goes horribly wrong. The heart of the song was never the fights. It was the moment Edward, bleeding on that floor, gives his own arm to bind his brother's soul to a suit of armor.

Research before rhymes

Before writing a single line, we went back to the story and checked the moments that matter: the failed attempt to bring back their mother, Ed losing a leg and Al his whole body, the blood seal on the armor, Al trading his soul to give Ed his arm back, and Ed finally giving up his own Gate — his alchemy — as the right answer to equivalent exchange.

Each moment became an image the lyrics could carry without naming anyone:

  • a chalk circle on a cellar floor;
  • a door that always takes a toll;
  • “a little flame in brother's blood” for the blood seal;
  • a hollow knight who cannot sleep, cannot feel, cannot weep;
  • a childhood home set alight so there is no road back.

The album's rules

All the songs follow the same constraints: no proper nouns, no lines borrowed from the source, six to eight syllables per line, rhymes in pairs. The chorus is a question that Truth asks again and again:

Every gift comes through my door
So what will you trade?
A leg, an arm, a soul, and more
Before the debt is paid

In the last chorus, the question turns into its answer: “There's nothing left to trade… and every debt is paid.” The song ends with the two brothers walking in the sun, one leg still made of steel.

Not a mocking god

The first instinct was to let Truth sneer, like a croupier at a rigged table. It didn't feel right. The final narrator is a grave witness, not a bully: the song puts the weight on what those two boys endured, and on how stubbornly they kept going.

Finding the sound — and leaving J-rock behind

After several J-rock tracks on the album, this one needed another color. Three directions were on the table: a dark cabaret waltz, a Slavic folk orchestral ballad (a nod to the series' Russian-language song Bratja), and an industrial “steampunk” sound. The compass came from an outside reference: the epic electro-pop of Royalty by Egzod, Maestro Chives & Neoni.

The final recipe: dark cinematic electro-pop at 82 BPM in half-time, in E minor. It opens on a solo cello and a music box, a Slavic folk violin carries the melody, sub-bass drops hit after each chorus — and a single hollow armor clang rings out before every chorus. The bridge falls almost silent, with just the cello.

One rhyme too many

An early bridge said “saved… save… saved” in two lines. Three times the same word is a lot, even for a song about debts. Small fix, big difference: the bridge now answers an earlier line about the chain that binds Al's soul.

How it was made

The lyrics were written by OKalme. The music and the vocal were generated with Suno, using our own voice. We'd rather tell you than have you guess — that's a different game.

The cover follows the album's ink-and-glitch style: an old balance scale with an iron hand on one side and a small flame on the other, in amber gold. No characters, just the trade.
